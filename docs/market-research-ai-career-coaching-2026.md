# The Compass Owns a White Space It Cannot Yet Keep

**Executive summary.** As of November 2026 nobody in Australia, and almost nobody globally, sells what The Compass by Purposefields does: a free, step-by-step, self-authored career-*direction* journey with a non-directive AI thinking partner aimed at everyday workers rather than graduates or office professionals. The AI career-coaching market has split into a crowded, venture-funded job-search execution tier (Teal, Careerflow, Kickresume, LinkedIn Career Coach, Indeed Career Scout, which **launched in Australia in July 2026**), an enterprise tier retreating from consumers (BetterUp **stopped new individual sign-ups on 1 Feb 2026**; Valence raised a **US$50M Series B**), scored assessment vendors selling US$9-60 PDF reports, and a thin direction tier that is philanthropically funded (CareerVillage Coach), a Big-Tech experiment (Google Career Dreamer, US-only) or youth-only (Australia's Anyway/Year13, launched April 2026 with Microsoft). Australia's own government tools (myfuture, Your Career) are questionnaire-to-occupation matchers with no conversational AI, and **44% of working Australians are open to a career change while only 7% act**, which is precisely the hesitant population a direction tool serves. Against that backdrop The Compass's coaching method, safety design and editorial craft are above market norm, but the product is pre-commercial on every axis and is a one-shot worksheet: no landing page, no return visit, no reality-testing, no practitioner view, no analytics, and five visible bugs within two minutes of first use (a literal "null" on step 2 among them). The evidence on effective career interventions (Brown & Ryan Krane 2000; Whiston et al. 2017) says the ingredients that move outcomes are written exercises, individualised feedback, world-of-work information, modelling, support-building and repeated sessions; The Compass nails the first two and skips the other four. The recommendations below therefore fix the bugs and onboarding in days, add a "Find out" prototyping step, a printable map and a scheduled return visit in weeks, and bet over months on a consent-based practitioner mode for Workforce Australia providers, TAFEs and Career Transition Assistance, timed to the **$312m employment-services rebuild rolling out from late 2026**. A caveat runs through the whole report: every competitor fact below rests on search-result snippets because the research session could not fetch any vendor, app-store or government page; prices and feature claims should be re-verified before external use.

## Five segments, and the one The Compass sits in is almost empty

The market map is clearer than the noise suggests. The first and largest segment is **job-search execution** suites that have bolted an "AI career coach" onto résumé builders and trackers. Teal offers a free-forever tier plus Teal+ at roughly **US$13/week or US$29/month with no annual plan**, and reviewers describe "feature sprawl" and AI content that "feels forced" ([FrontDeskReview](https://frontdeskreview.com/software/resume-builders/teal.md); [Scoutify](https://scoutify.com/blog/teal-review)). Careerflow charges **US$8.99/week or US$23.99/month** and sells B2B lines to coaches, bootcamps and workforce agencies ([Careerflow blog](https://www.careerflow.ai/blog/best-ai-resume-builders); [Careerflow for coaches](https://careerflow.ai/organizations/career-coaches)). Kickresume's own help centre concedes its coach "functions more like a conversational chatbot that answers your questions rather than a true coaching assistant that guides you and visually maps out your potential next steps" ([Kickresume help center](https://kickresume.com/en/help-center/5-best-ai-career-coaches/)). LinkedIn's Career Coach requires Premium at **US$29.99/month or more** and is judged "less capable than ChatGPT for open-ended career conversations" ([itsourcecode](https://itsourcecode.com/linkedin/is-linkedin-premium-worth-it/); [CO/AI](https://about.getcoai.com/news/how-linkedin-premiums-ai-features-promise-more-than-they-deliver)). The Australian entrant here is Indeed Career Scout, free and mobile-only, which arrived for Australian jobseekers in **July 2026** with pathways, expected earnings and mock interviews, personalised from profile and "how you interact with Indeed" ([IT Brief Australia](https://itbrief.com.au/story/indeed-launches-ai-career-scout-for-australian-jobseekers); [eCommerceNews AU](https://ecommercenews.com.au/story/indeed-launches-ai-career-scout-for-australian-jobseekers)). Every product in this tier starts at occupations or job ads; none starts at the person's life.

The second segment is **scored assessment** vendors. CareerExplorer, Truity, 16Personalities, CliftonStrengths and VIA sell a free teaser and a paid report: Truity around **US$29**, 16Personalities' Career Suite **US$29**, CliftonStrengths Top 5 **US$19.99** and the full 34 **US$49.99-59.99** ([resume.io](https://resume.io/blog/career-aptitude-tests); [16Personalities](https://www.16personalities.com/academy/logician); [Gallup PDF](https://www.gallup.com/file/cliftonstrengths/en/266789/CliftonStrengths%2034%20and%20Top%205%20Comparison%20(1).pdf)). Pigment sells a 120-question forced-choice test and a 36-page PDF for **US$49.99-99.99** ([StackSocial](https://www.stacksocial.com/sales/career-self-discovery-assessment-one-time-purchase); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/fr/article/pigment-career-self-discovery-assessment/)). AI arrives here as a chat layer over the profile: Gallup AI "automatically incorporates your CliftonStrengths results into every response" but only inside a Gallup Access subscription ([Gallup support](https://support.gallup.com/hc/en-us/articles/48408264183827-Gallup-AI)). The dominant one-star complaint across Truity, 16Personalities, CareerHunter and Apt AI is a paywall revealed after 15-20 minutes of effort ([Trustpilot Truity](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/truity.com?page=2); [Trustpilot CareerHunter](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/careerhunter.io); [Trustpilot tryapt.ai](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tryapt.ai?page=3)). Hiring-side "career matching" apps (pymetrics, Traitify, Good&Co, Plum) all migrated to employer screening; consumer direction-finding did not sustain them.

The third segment is **enterprise and hybrid coaching**, which is where the capital is. Valence's Nadia raised a **US$50M Series B** in September 2025, took an ADP Ventures investment in 2026 and shipped Microsoft 365 integration in May 2026 ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/valence-50-million-series-b-raised-for-enterprise-ai-coach-platform/amp/); [Valence](https://valence.co/blog/valence-microsoft-365-may-2026)). BetterUp launched Grow (AI-only) in January 2025 and then **closed individual coaching to new customers on 1 Feb 2026**, ending it entirely on 31 Jan 2027 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250121963707/en); [Coach Training EDU](https://www.coachtrainingedu.com/blog/betterup-individual-coaching-ending/)). The Conference Board's October 2025 report concludes AI can handle about **90% of day-to-day career coaching** but recommends explicit criteria to escalate "emotional distress or critical career decisions" to a human ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/ai-can-provide-most-career-coaching-but-still-need-humans/803962/)). This tier is retreating from consumers, which leaves ordinary workers uncontested by premium players.

The fourth segment is **public and non-profit career information**: O*NET/My Next Move, the UK National Careers Service, 80,000 Hours, and in Australia myfuture (**800,000+ registered users**, nine-activity interests/skills/values profile) and Your Career (Career Quiz and Skills Match, account required) ([Technology Decisions](https://www.technologydecisions.com.au/content/futureed/news/career-information-site-streamlined-634779515); [myGov](https://my.gov.au/en/services/education/upgrading-professional-skills-or-qualifications/changing-your-career)). These are strong on labour-market data and weak on conversation; no source documents generative AI in any Australian government tool. The only free AI-coach with Australian scale is Anyway (formerly Year13), launched in **April 2026** with Microsoft, aimed at **14-24 year olds** via up to 1,000 schools ([ChannelLife](https://channellife.com.au/story/year13-launches-anyway-ai-career-coach-in-australia); [Microsoft Source Asia](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/2026/04/23/microsoft-announces-australias-largest-ai-skilling-commitment/)). Melbourne's Getmee (**AU$2.5m raised**) white-labels employability coaching to Y Careers and MTC Australia's "CoachMe" ([Business News Australia](https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/melbourne-startup-getmee-raises--2-5m-for-ai-coaching-service.html); [MTC Australia](https://www.mtcaustralia.com.au/blog/mtc-australia-launches-mtc-coachme/)).

The fifth segment, **direction and self-discovery with AI**, is where The Compass lives, and it is sparsely populated. CareerVillage's Coach (launched August 2024, Gates **US$1M**, DeLuca **US$1.14M**, Schultz-led **US$3M** initiative) is free, US-centric and tracks outcomes with the validated CAAS-SF survey ([Schultz Family Foundation](https://schultzfamilyfoundation.org/news-release/coach-launches-first-of-its-kind-ai-career-development-tool-built-for-equity-and-measurable-results); [Gates Foundation](https://gatesfoundation.org/about/committed-grants/2024/02/inv-066023)). Google Career Dreamer (February 2025) is free, exploratory and US-only ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/19/googles-career-dreamer-uses-ai-to-help-you-explore-job-possibilities/)). Marlee runs a 48-trait motivation assessment with 8-week programs on a free Starter tier but has drifted toward team and leadership positioning and shows zero reviews on three directories ([Marlee FAQ](https://getmarlee.com/faq/what-does-marlee-measure); [Krowdbase](https://www.krowdbase.com/software/coach-marlee)). Rocky.ai offers daily five-minute solution-focused check-ins with memory ([Rocky help](https://help.rocky.ai/ai-chat/why-your-ai-coach-asks-you-always-questions)). Sapia's Phai, built on Claude, found in a 1,000-user trial that **63% sought a change of direction** and that strengths, confidence and clarity dominated the questions, the strongest external evidence that demand for direction coaching is real and skews mid-career ([CFOtech UK](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/sapia-ai-unveils-phai-an-ai-coach-for-career-change)). No product in this segment combines guided, user-authored steps with a non-directive AI partner for adults in Australia. That white space is real, and it is also a warning: venture money avoids it because consumer willingness to pay for pure direction-finding is unproven, and the products that survive are grant-funded or platform-subsidised.

## The evidence favours written reflection and individual feedback, then demands reality-testing and repetition

The research base on career interventions is unusually consistent and it cuts both ways for The Compass. Brown and Ryan Krane's meta-analysis of 62 studies (mean effect size **0.34**) identified five ingredients that each predict better outcomes and that add linearly: written exercises, individualised interpretation and feedback, world-of-work information, modelling, and building support for choices in one's social network ([CareerVision summary](https://careervision.org/critical-ingredients-career-interventions/); [FSU tech report](https://career.fsu.edu/sites/g/files/imported/storage/original/application/1bcbeffae96901d7661dc5fd160b7a74.pdf)). Whiston and colleagues' 2017 replication across 57 studies (weighted mean **0.352**) added counsellor support and values clarification as critical, and across these reviews repeated sessions beat one-offs and the largest effects land on career decision-making self-efficacy ([EconBiz](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/effectiveness-of-career-choice-interventions-a-meta-analytic-replication-and-extension/10011720945); [CDAA best-practice summary](https://cdaa.imiscloud.com/Web/Blog/Posts/What-is-best-practice-in-careers-education--An-outline-of-the-evidence.aspx)).

Mapped onto the ten steps, The Compass is strong where software usually is weak. Every step is a written exercise. The AI reflects the person's own words, two to five sentences at a time, which is closer to individualised feedback than any PDF report. Steps one to three are values clarification done more deeply than any scored matcher. The constraint step (pay floor, hours, stress, mode, location) is a soft form of reality-testing applied to the person rather than to the market, and nothing surveyed has an equivalent. The ideation device (random know-plus-do-plus-love trios) is a software version of Designing Your Life's Mind Maps, and the three sub-two-hour actions scheduled into a calendar are an implementation-intention device most competitors lack; they stop at a report.

The weaknesses are equally clear. **World-of-work information is excluded by design**, and the recognised stance among reflective frameworks is not to withhold it but to defer it: Designing Your Life's prototyping "means gathering information through conversations and experiences", and Ibarra's Working Identity insists that "the more unfamiliar the new possibilities, the more necessary it becomes to learn about them through direct involvement rather than planning" ([HKUST syllabus](https://seng.hkust.edu.hk/sites/default/files/IMCE/UG/Course%20Syllabus/Fall_2023-2024/ENGG1400_Fall%202023-24.pdf); [Careershifters](https://www.careershifters.org/book-reviews/herminia-ibarra-working-identity)). Public tools build the reality check in: O*NET filters by Job Zone and flags Bright Outlook occupations, and the UK National Careers Service links every match to pay, hours and routes in ([DOL TEN 30-10](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ETA/advisories/TEN/2011/TEN30-10.pdf); [NCS explore careers](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk:443/explore-careers)). **Modelling** (seeing someone like you in the role, the heart of Roadtrip Nation's video library) and **support-building** (telling a partner or friend, Designing Your Life's "team") are absent. And **repeat sessions**, the ingredient the meta-analyses rate highest, are impossible without any return path. Two 2026 randomised trials sharpen this last point: a preregistered three-arm RCT (N=517) found an AI coach improved goal progress versus control (**d=0.33**), attributing the effect to perceived social accountability, and a chatbot career-coaching RCT (N=82) reduced negative affect over three weeks via career-goal clarity ([arXiv 2603.17887](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2603.17887v1); [FAU CRIS](https://cris.fau.de/publications/365286850?lang=de_DE)). Accountability is the active ingredient The Compass gives away at the "Copy my map" screen. The same literature cautions against over-claiming: the best head-to-head RCT (N=114) found substantial effects only for accredited human coaching, so "a structured thinking partner that helps you clarify and act" is supportable messaging and "as good as a coach" is not ([PDF](https://a.storyblok.com/f/45434/x/8a08babd23/research_ai_vs_human_coaching.pdf)).

## Candid assessment: disciplined craft wrapped around a one-shot worksheet

The audit ran the app without an API key, so coaching quality is judged from prompts and flow; `npm test` passed **7/7** API-level tests and the walkthrough produced **zero console or page errors** on desktop and mobile. The headline is that The Compass is a tightly scoped, well-crafted single-session reflection worksheet with an AI partner bolted alongside, and that is both its strength and its ceiling.

**Strengths.** The coaching method is coherent and unusually disciplined. The system prompt forbids the universal LLM failure mode of over-advising: "Never tell them what to choose", "Never give your opinion on whether an idea is realistic, achievable, well paid or in demand", and suggestions may use "ONLY their own words" (`lib/prompts.js` lines 8-11, 20). The ideation task explicitly widens the field to trades, care, community, outdoors, government and self-employment, which is real evidence behind the "everyday people" positioning. Every chat turn carries the person's full answer snapshot, so the coach is grounded in their words. The register is plain Australian English with a banned-jargon list, two to five sentences and at most one question. Friction design is above market: "Still needed: choose 5 more values" in the pinned bar instead of greyed buttons, two-tap confirmation on destructive actions, autosave on every keystroke, Enter-to-add, arrow-button fallback for drag ranking, and sensible calendar defaults producing Google Calendar deep links and RFC 5545 .ics files with alarms. The visual identity (Newsreader serif, IBM Plex Mono eyebrows, teal, 720px measure, working dark mode) reads as editorial rather than SaaS and separates the product from the blue-gradient LinkedIn-clone aesthetic of most AI career tools. Trust and safety are handled more explicitly than anything documented in the consumer market: a four-point consent screen, two-layer crisis detection with five Australian services including 13YARN, prompts and API key server-side, strict CSP, per-IP rate limiting, refusal fallback surfaced honestly, and graceful degradation when AI is off. The crisis dialog already exceeds Mindframe's norm of listing at least two 24/7 services with direct links ([Mindframe guidelines](https://mindframe.org.au/mental-health/communicating-about-mental-ill-health/mindframe-guidelines)). Engineering quality is high for ~1,070 lines: zero-build, NDJSON streaming, structured JSON-schema outputs, mapped error codes.

**Weaknesses, starting with the bugs a first-time user will hit.** On step 2 ("Who are you?") a **literal "null" renders under the heading** on desktop and mobile because `heading(i,"Who are you?")` has no lead and `w.append(...heading(...))` stringifies the null third element (`public/app.js` lines 155 and 183). "I am" statements are not case-normalised, so typing "Learner" yields "I am a Learner" in the summary (`iam()` line 79). The summary's Values line collapses into one run-on sentence because items are joined with spaces that HTML collapses, while the "I am" block directly below correctly uses `white-space:pre-line` (line 389). Strengths-card placeholders truncate to "e.g. Gardening, N" at the 210px column minimum. After adding a strength the workspace scrolls so the h2 is clipped under the header (no `scroll-margin`). The crisis dialog closes only via a button (no Esc), neither modal traps focus, and the `--faint` hint colour sits at roughly **2.6:1 contrast**, below AA for small text. Two safety nits matter more than they look: keyword detection is substring-based, so "I can't go on in this job" will false-positive, and when the dialog opens **the message is still sent to Claude** because `send()` does not pause (lines 417-418).

The structural weaknesses are larger. There is **no landing page**: `/` opens straight into a four-checkbox consent modal over step 1, with no statement of what the Compass is, who it is for, how long it takes (a realistic **40-60 minutes in one sitting**) or what you get; the only positioning copy is the `<meta description>`. Every competitor found has a marketing page and a free-entry call to action, and the market's harshest reviews go to products that extract effort before disclosing the deal ([Trustpilot tryapt.ai](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tryapt.ai?page=3)). The red "Help now" button is the most visually prominent control on every screen, framing a career tool as mental-health-adjacent before the first question is read. On a 390px phone, header plus pinned bar consume **167px + 64px, about 27% of the viewport**, tap targets sit at 31-37px against the 44px guideline, and the coach lives in a hidden "Talk it through" tab, so phone users effectively never meet the coach unless they go looking. The Mix step runs to **~2,330px on desktop and five-plus screens on mobile** of near-identical cards (3x3x3 means duplicate trios are inevitable) before reaching "My ideas". Pay is a required annual pre-tax figure with no hourly, weekly or "not sure" option, a poor fit for award-wage workers. The "What I need" constraints are collected and then never applied to any idea. The coach never initiates; its intros are static strings, and it cannot see someone stuck on a four-word North Star. Retention is nil: state lives in three localStorage keys, one of which (`purposefields_goals`) nothing reads; the only re-engagement is a calendar event description that points nowhere. Commercially the product is pre-everything: no analytics or error reporting (the team cannot see where people abandon), no privacy policy, terms, data-retention statement or ABN, no B2B or practitioner surface, and an uncontrolled cost profile (Opus-class model with `max_tokens: 8000` for every chat and three of four suggestion tasks, no per-user budget, no prompt caching despite a stable system prompt and profile). The front end is a single 473-line IIFE with one global state object; adding a step touches six places by hand and there are no front-end tests.

## Gap analysis: where the market and the evidence are ahead of us, and where we are ahead of them

Against the market, the gaps cluster into five. **Return and accountability**: Rocky, Marlee, 80,000 Hours and Gallup all keep the person coming back; even the UK National Careers Service lets users save results by email or code without an account ([Oak Park guide](https://oakspark.greenhousecms.co.uk/National-Careers-Service)). **An external read on the person**: Marlee's 48 traits, Pigment's 82, Gallup's 34 themes and Indeed's hiring data give users a sense of being seen rather than echoed; several listicles treat this as the differentiator over ChatGPT, and the recurring "validates whatever I lean toward" critique of general chatbots is one The Compass's non-directive stance could attract ([pigment.is](https://pigment.is/blogs/blogs/best-ai-for-career-advice)). **A result artefact**: the category norm is a 10-plus-page PDF or a shareable profile; our copy-as-text map is below baseline. **Positioning surface**: a tagline, a landing page, trust facts and a mobile presence. **Distribution hooks**: every scaled direction tool reaches everyday people through intermediaries (CareerVillage through institutions, Anyway through schools, Getmee through providers, Quinncia through universities) and each needs accounts, a practitioner view, reporting and branding, none of which exist.

Against the evidence, the gaps are reality-testing by the person's own enquiry, modelling, support-building and repeated sessions, as set out above. The Australian context sharpens two of these. The OECD found that **74% of highly educated versus 44% of low-educated mid-career Australians** had used career guidance, the widest gap in its sample, that rural adults use it less, and that employed adults not at risk of job loss generally cannot access publicly subsidised guidance ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/strengthening-career-guidance-for-mid-career-adults-in-australia_e08803ce-en/full-report/)). Human coaching costs about **A$195/hour on average (Sydney ~A$295), with eight-session programs near A$2,450** ([Bark](https://www.bark.com/en/au/business-growth-hub/business-career-coaching-cost/)), roughly a day's pay per session for award-wage workers. A free, no-account, phone-first tool in plain English is structurally suited to exactly the people the system misses; the gap is that it cannot yet reach them (no intermediaries) or keep them (no return).

Where The Compass is ahead is worth stating plainly because it should go on the landing page. It is the only direction-first, non-directive, step-by-step tool found; it is the only one with a whole-of-life constraints step; it ends in dated actions rather than a report; its privacy model (no accounts, no server storage) is stronger than any documented consumer competitor in a climate where chat logs are retained, used for training and subpoenable ([WDBJ7](https://www.wdbj7.com/2026/04/27/ai-chatbot-privacy-concerns-grow-users-share-personal-details/)); and its consent, crisis and register design map directly onto the APA's November 2025 advisory (chatbots as "supportive adjunct, not substitute") and the ICF's AI Coaching Framework domains ([APA advisory](https://apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-chatbots-wellness-apps); [ICF framework](https://coachingfederation.org/resource/icf-artificial-intelligence-ai-coaching-framework-and-standards)). The one trust gap is ICF domain five, assurance and testing: prompts have no versioning, eval set or logging.

## Conclusion

The research reframes The Compass's deliberate choices as a positioning asset with an expiry date. Being direction-first, non-directive and account-less is genuinely unoccupied ground in Australia; Indeed and SEEK will own "which job should I apply for", Anyway owns school leavers, and the government tools own occupation data, so the whole-of-life, everyday-worker lane is open. But the same choices that make the product trustworthy (no storage, no judgement, no accounts) are what stop it from doing the things the evidence says matter most: reality-testing by the person's own enquiry, support from other people, and coming back. The fix is not to abandon the stance and inject labour-market scores, which would put The Compass in a fight with Indeed it cannot win; it is to deliver reality-testing as the person's own "Find out" homework, and repetition as a scheduled return the coach initiates.

The commercial implication is sharper still. No consumer direction tool has scaled on subscriptions, and the premium coaching platforms are leaving individuals behind, so The Compass's path to everyday Australians runs through intermediaries who already have the relationship: Workforce Australia providers moving to Employment Goal Plans from late 2026, CTA deliverers for over-45s, TAFEs enrolling 851,000-plus Fee-Free students, and councils and libraries in the regions where guidance access is thinnest. Every one of those buyers will ask for three things the product does not have today: a way for a practitioner to see the map with the person's consent, evidence that it changes something, and a privacy posture that survives the December 2026 ADM rules. Building those, on top of a bug-free, honestly introduced ten-step journey, converts a well-made worksheet into the only Australian career-direction coach that respects the person and still gets them to act.
