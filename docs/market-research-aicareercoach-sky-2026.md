# Sky Out-Coaches the Market but Isn't Ready to Charge

**Executive summary (BLUF).** As of November 2026, AI Career Coach (aicareercoach.net, branch `claude/fervent-gates-ejr1dq`) is a more serious coaching product than any consumer competitor we could find: Sky is the only AI coach in the set with a stated coaching school (ACT, solution-focused, self-determination theory), nine protocolised focuses, a transparent memory the member can read, edit, export and crypto-shred, a periodic self-review that hunts for flattery and generic advice, honesty-enforced résumé and cover-letter writers, and a human coach bookable in-app. On that basis it beats Teal, Careerflow, Kickresume, LinkedIn Career Coach and Indeed Career Scout on coaching depth and trust design, and beats Rocky.ai and Marlee on memory control and career tooling. But it is **not ready to take money**: the footer links to `/privacy` and `/terms` 404 on every page, the `start_trial` ad conversion never fires, a failed card at day 7 keeps full access until day 30, the legacy vikashan booking API still accepts unauthenticated A$65 to A$250 payments, every member email is branded "new page", and a member who uses the advertised caps costs roughly **A$75 to A$80 in model spend against a A$25 price**. Against the evidence on effective career interventions (Brown & Ryan Krane 2000; Whiston et al. 2017; the 2026 AI-coaching RCTs), Sky delivers written exercises, individualised feedback and values clarification unusually well but still skips world-of-work information, modelling and support-building, and, despite the memory, has no accountability rhythm: no daily or weekly check-in, no "how did that task go?", and chat history that resets whenever the member switches focus. The Australian context sharpens the opportunity: A$25 a month sits below Teal, Careerflow and LinkedIn Premium and at about an eighth of one human hour (Bark's A$195 average), the OECD's widest-in-sample guidance gap (74% of high-educated vs 44% of low-educated mid-career Australians) is exactly the hesitant everyday-worker population Sky's register suits, and the A$312m Workforce Australia rebuild rolling out from late 2026 opens a provider channel. The recommendations below separate launch blockers (legal pages, payment-failure handling, rebrand, legacy endpoints, cost controls, crisis-detection fixes), quick wins (a dozen file:line bugs), near-term product changes (an accountability loop, a "Find out" reality-testing step, memory that learns from the writers, mental-health screening) and strategic bets (provider and TAFE mode timed to the Employment Goal Plan, outcome measurement, a free tier). One caveat runs through the whole report: every competitor fact rests on search-result snippets because the research session could not fetch any vendor, app-store or government page, while every fact about our own product comes from a first-hand code audit and live walkthrough of commit 27ca6ac.

## The market has split into five tiers, and we now sit in three of them

The November 2026 landscape has five recognisable segments, and the pivot from the free standalone Compass to a paid membership moved our product from the emptiest segment into the two most contested ones while keeping a foot in the first.

The **job-search execution tier** is the largest and best funded. Kickresume's own help centre admits its coach "functions more like a conversational chatbot that answers your questions rather than a true coaching assistant that guides you and visually maps out your potential next steps" ([Kickresume help center](https://kickresume.com/en/help-center/5-best-ai-career-coaches/)). LinkedIn Career Coach needs Premium Career at **US$29.99 a month or more** and is judged "less capable than ChatGPT for open-ended career conversations" ([itsourcecode](https://itsourcecode.com/linkedin/is-linkedin-premium-worth-it/); [CO/AI](https://about.getcoai.com/news/how-linkedin-premiums-ai-features-promise-more-than-they-deliver)). The positioning consequence is that **no competitor spans direction, coaching with memory, execution writers and a human coach in one membership**; that breadth is our genuine differentiator and also our cost problem. The two products that come closest to the "whole person" promise are grant-funded (CareerVillage) or enterprise (BetterUp, Valence), and the two with Australian reach are free (Indeed Career Scout, Anyway). Every named consumer competitor has a free-forever tier; we have a 24-hour demo with ten messages.

## The evidence rewards Sky's written, individualised, values-first design and penalises its missing loop

Two meta-analyses anchor what works. Brown and Ryan Krane's review of 62 studies (mean effect size **0.34**) identified five ingredients that each predict better outcomes and add linearly: written exercises, individualised interpretation and feedback, world-of-work information, modelling, and building support for choices in one's social network ([CareerVision summary](https://careervision.org/critical-ingredients-career-interventions/); [FSU tech report](https://career.fsu.edu/sites/g/files/imported/storage/original/application/1bcbeffae96901d7661dc5fd160b7a74.pdf)). Whiston and colleagues' 2017 replication across 57 studies (weighted mean **0.352**) added counsellor support and values clarification as critical, and across these reviews repeated sessions beat one-offs and the largest effects land on career decision-making self-efficacy ([EconBiz](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/effectiveness-of-career-choice-interventions-a-meta-analytic-replication-and-extension/10011720945); [CDAA best-practice summary](https://cdaa.imiscloud.com/Web/Blog/Posts/What-is-best-practice-in-careers-education--An-outline-of-the-evidence.aspx)). Three 2026 trials add the AI-specific evidence. A preregistered three-arm RCT (N=517) found an AI coach improved goal progress versus control (**d=0.33**) and attributed the effect to perceived social accountability ([arXiv 2603.17887](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2603.17887v1)). A chatbot career-coaching RCT (N=82) reduced negative affect over three weeks via career-goal clarity ([FAU CRIS](https://cris.fau.de/publications/365286850?lang=de_DE)). And the best head-to-head RCT (N=114) found substantial effects only for accredited human coaching, so "as good as a coach" is not supportable messaging while "a structured thinking partner that helps you clarify and act" is ([HRDI PDF](https://a.storyblok.com/f/45434/x/8a08babd23/research_ai_vs_human_coaching.pdf)). The Conference Board's survey of workers who used AI coaching found 91% would use it again but named "inconsistent contextual memory" and "scripted language" as the weaknesses ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/ai-can-provide-most-career-coaching-but-still-need-humans/803962/)).

Mapped onto Sky's nine focuses and the tools, the picture is uneven in an instructive way.

That is an unusual and fixable profile: the gaps are mostly product loops, not model capability.

## Candid assessment: the best-designed coaching product in the set, shipped with a storefront that isn't finished

Both audits ran the full test suites (**92/92 Node, 22/22 Python**) and captured 39 screenshots at desktop and phone widths with the API mocked. The headline is that the coaching core is mature and the commercial, legal and operational shell around it is not.

**Strengths.** The methodology is coherent: a written principles summary ("strengths-based and person-centred… The member sets the direction; you ask, reflect and offer… Never promise outcomes") is injected every turn (`lib/newpage-principles.js:26`), and a second model call every fifth and tenth message looks for "flattery, promised outcomes… prescribing instead of asking" and "Drifting, spinning, giving generic advice" (`lib/newpage-principles.js:58,71`), which targets precisely the "robot factor" and "textbook correct and operationally useless" complaints the market makes about AI coaches ([Compono](https://www.heycompono.com/blog/ai-coaching-reviews-guide); [Noomii](https://orgs.noomii.com/i-trusted-ai-coaching-and-paid-the-price)). Memory control exceeds anything documented: every note is visible with Edit and delete, a "Let Sky keep notes" toggle, JSON export and two-tap crypto-shredding (`app/context.js`, `lib/newpage-memory-store.js:162`), with AES-256-GCM envelope encryption, AAD bound to member and field, and key rotation (`lib/newpage-crypto.js`). Honesty is enforced in code, not just promised: `cleanTailor` forces roles, dates, employers and qualifications back to the base résumé (`lib/newpage-tailor.js:81–127`), the Python agent's `lint.py` flags any figure or employer absent from the candidate's materials, and approval gates are server-signed (`api/newpage/resume.py:13–15`). The cover-letter flow (ad → competencies → résumé gaps → pointed questions → draft → recruiter review → revise) is the best-designed writing loop in the comparison set and directly answers the "generic AI cover letter" complaint. Crisis handling runs in code before the model on every message with a stored reply and visible support card (`lib/newpage-app.js:38–49`, `app/app.js:421`). Price framing is honest three times over before the FAQ: "Free for 7 days · then $25 a month · cancel in two taps", "Nothing charged today. We email you three days before the trial ends", caps listed, "No annual trap" (`index.html:102,161–173`), which is the opposite of the weekly-billing nudges and hidden paywalls reviewers punish ([Trustpilot tryapt.ai](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tryapt.ai?page=3)). Checkout is server-priced, webhook-idempotent and signature-verified, and the post-membership memory page with 30-day and 12-month retention, reminders and self-serve delete is well done (`lib/newpage-retention.js:12`, `memory/memory.js:24–44`). Brand execution is faithful and accessible: en-AU, 44 px targets, focus management, no emoji, no dark patterns.

**Weaknesses that block taking money.** The footer on every page links `/privacy` and `/terms`, and no file or route exists (`index.html:196`, `join/index.html:64`, `demo/index.html:50`, `memory/index.html:18`, `app/index.html:33`); there is no refund policy, ABN, legal entity or contact address anywhere public. The `start_trial` conversion is never fired because `join/join.js` never calls `window.track` and the join button has no `data-track` attribute (`track.js:2,29,31`, `join/index.html:46`), so the README's claim is false and conversion-based ad bidding cannot work. There is no handler for `invoice.payment_failed`, `past_due` or `charge.refunded` (`api/webhooks/stripe.js:46–57`), and the initial access grant is `'1m'`, thirty days, for a seven-day trial (`lib/newpage-payments.js:32`), so a card that fails on day 7 keeps full caps until day 30 and a refund does not revoke access. Every member email and the unsubscribe page are branded "new page / BY PURPOSEFIELDS" (`lib/newpage-emails.js:39–40`, `api/newpage/unsubscribe.js:33`) and `og.png` is the old New Page quiz with a `vikashan.com.au` URL (`index.html:10`). The legacy vikashan booking engine is still deployed: unauthenticated `api/packages/create.js` and `api/packages/setup-plan.js` create Stripe PaymentIntents and attach cards at A$65 to A$250 tiers with instalment plans (`lib/booking-price.js:7–9`). Program-era copy survives in live paths: the launch email promising "$500 for the four weeks… full refund… apply for a scholarship" is wired to an admin button (`lib/newpage-emails.js:184–199`), the welcome email carries the old refund line (`:244`), and the pre-JS join page shows a $500 four-week program with a refund guarantee (`join/index.html:32,38–47`).

**Weaknesses in economics and operations.** Caps cost about **A$75–80 per member-month at the limits, three times the A$25 price** (300 × ~A$0.025 Sky messages + 150 × ~A$0.22 tailored résumés + 100 × ~A$0.30 letters + 3 × ~A$2 résumés), while a typical engaged member costs A$6–7; the same caps apply during the free week; there is no `cache_control` anywhere despite a 2.5–3.5K-token system prompt and 40-message history re-sent every turn (`lib/newpage-usage.js:6`, `lib/newpage-coach.js:20,151,156`). Usage is recorded before the AI call, so a failed generation consumes allowance, and the counter is a non-atomic read-then-upsert (`api/newpage/app.js:135–140`, `lib/newpage-usage.js:33–38`). Rate limits and the admin lockout are in-memory per serverless instance and reset on cold start (`lib/security.js:1–45`), so the 5-demo-starts-per-hour limit is cosmetic on Vercel and the anonymous demo can burn roughly A$25–30 a day. The landing page says "Everything Sky learns is encrypted with a key just for you" (`index.html:156,181`) while generated résumés, cover letters, Compass answers, tasks and check-ins sit in plaintext (`api/newpage/app.js:56–58`), and `lib/newpage-crypto.js:232–233` itself says the privacy page should state this is not end-to-end encryption. "Delete everything" leaves the member row, docs, tasks, Compass and Stripe customer in place (`api/newpage/app.js:700–704`). Under the Privacy Act the gaps are specific: no APP 1 privacy policy, no APP 5 collection notice at the join form (`join/index.html:33–48`), no APP 8 disclosure that data is processed by Vercel, Supabase, Anthropic, Resend and Stripe, no statement that chats are not used for training (Anthropic's API defaults would let us say so), and no way to meet the automated-decision-making transparency requirement that applies to decisions from **10 December 2026**, with civil penalties up to **$78,200 per penalty-unit tier** ([Hamilton Locke](https://hamiltonlocke.com.au/transparency-in-automated-decision-making-what-regulated-entities-need-to-know-and-do-before-december-2026/); [Gilbert + Tobin](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/automated-decision-making-transparency-under-the-privacy-act); [OAIC guidance](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/guidance-on-privacy-and-the-use-of-commercially-available-ai-products)).

**Weaknesses in the coaching product itself.** The crisis keyword list is a substring match, so "suicide prevention officer" in a job title triggers the crisis card instead of coaching while "no point going on", "can't do this anymore" and "want to disappear" are not caught (`lib/newpage-app.js:38–46`); the landing page lists no crisis number at all although BRAND §9 and Mindframe's standard ask for two 24/7 services with direct links (`index.html:195`; [Mindframe](https://mindframe.org.au/mental-health/communicating-about-mental-ill-health/mindframe-guidelines)). Four focuses (CBT, Reducing the charge, Recovery, Executive function) run therapy-adjacent protocols with prompt-only safeguards, no screening question, and no pause on repeated low-mood check-ins (`app/app.js:563`); the APA's November 2025 advisory warns that chatbots' ability "to safely guide someone experiencing crisis is limited and unpredictable" and calls them a "supportive adjunct, not substitute", and the founder of the Yara therapy app shut it down in November 2025 as "too dangerous" for serious issues ([APA advisory](https://apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-chatbots-wellness-apps); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/28/yara-ai-therapy-app-founder-shut-down-startup-decided-too-dangerous-serious-mental-health-issues)). Memory is loaded with `.limit(60)` while `MEMORY_LIMIT` is 80 and the UI says "of 80" (`api/newpage/app.js:85` vs `lib/newpage-context.js:142`), so notes 61–80 are stored and shown but never reach Sky, and Sky's own eviction check can never fire. `cleanCover` hard-codes `template: 'classic'` and ignores the member's chosen template, so "Edit the words → Save" resets the style (`lib/newpage-cover.js:308`). `cover_draft` and the final `doc_generate` are both counted as covers, so the three-step flow costs two of the hundred monthly letters (`api/newpage/app.js:138`). The writers are told to spell "programme" against BRAND §8 (`lib/newpage-tools.js:4`, `lib/newpage-cover.js:161`). Program residue reaches paying members: "your 60-minute session with him" in the Meet Sky drawer (`app/sky.js:47`), "Email me your coach's nudges during the four weeks" in the planner (`app/planner.js:431–434`), a Progress Report "for the end of their four weeks" (`lib/newpage-tools.js:155`), "open a template from your program" in Notes (`app/notes.js:509`), "Bring this to your session with your coach" on the Compass summary (`app/compass.js:243`), and "a AI Career Coach member" (`lib/newpage-coach.js:59`). The résumé writer shows the draft as a `<pre>` dump and the approved version as raw JSON with "Copy JSON" (`app/resume.js:258–261`); section documents download HTML with a `.doc` extension that modern Word warns on (`app/sections.js:127–128,184`). Memory notes and résumé text are injected into the system prompt without the "this is data, not instructions" framing the writers use (`lib/newpage-coach.js:70–71`). Navigation uses `history.replaceState`, so the browser back button leaves the app (`app/app.js:582`); the sticky composer overlaps the last message after send (`app/app.css:64`); `aria-live="polite"` on `<main>` announces every re-render (`app/index.html:30`); the Compass Mix step is a ~4,000 px lime-chipped scroll on a phone (`app/compass.js:121–124,158–165`).

The honest one-line verdict: Sky would win a side-by-side coaching comparison with every consumer product in the table, and the business around it would fail a basic pre-launch review by a payments provider, a privacy lawyer or an ad platform.

## Gaps against the market and the evidence, with the Australian context in front

Against the **execution tier**, the gaps are price-anchor and breadth. At A$25 a month (about US$16) we undercut Teal+ (US$29), LinkedIn Premium Career (US$29.99) and Careerflow (US$23.99) while being the only one without a free-forever tier, so a visitor's first comparison is against free Teal, free Kickresume, free Indeed Career Scout and free ChatGPT, and the 24-hour, ten-message demo is a thin answer to "competing with free", the reason Moxie's founders gave for closing ([Buzzsprout](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2411501/episodes/17723850-competing-with-free-why-we-closed-moxie)). We have no application tracker, no job board, no contacts CRM and no labour-market data, so the member who wants one place for the search will still open Teal or Indeed alongside us. Our advantages in this tier are honesty-enforced documents, the recruiter review loop and a coach that remembers the person rather than the file; none of those is on the landing page's proof list in those words.

Against the **memory tier**, the gap is rhythm. Rocky's daily five minutes and Marlee's weekly program are the retention mechanics, and the accountability RCT (d=0.33) says they are also the efficacy mechanic ([Rocky help](https://help.rocky.ai/ai-chat/why-your-ai-coach-asks-you-always-questions); [arXiv 2603.17887](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2603.17887v1)). Our lifecycle is welcome → trial-end warning → silence → memory reminders after the member leaves, with no usage nudge, no "you've used 80% of your messages", no win-back, no card-failed email and no post-cancel survey (`funnel_pricing_trust_ops_audit.md` §3). Memory itself is flat (80 notes, no summarisation, no recency weighting, no categories) and grows only when the model happens to call `save_memory` mid-reply; the Conference Board's "inconsistent contextual memory" complaint will apply the moment a member asks about something Sky noted 70 notes ago.

Against the **hybrid tier**, we are structurally well placed and operationally thin. BetterUp's exit leaves individuals with no premium human-plus-AI option; our A$250 hour, bookable in-app with a session brief, is the only consumer hybrid in the table. But A$250 sits above Bark's national average of **A$195 an hour ex GST** (Sydney A$295, Melbourne A$260, Brisbane A$225, Adelaide A$210, Perth A$205) and is shown without "inc GST", the coach is unnamed and uncredentialled on the site (`index.html:158`), there is no cancellation rule for a bought hour, and the Conference Board's escalation criteria (distress, critical decisions) exist in our prompts only as "only when it would genuinely help" rather than as a defined handoff ([Bark](https://www.bark.com/en/au/business-growth-hub/business-career-coaching-cost/); [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/ai-can-provide-most-career-coaching-but-still-need-humans/803962/)).

Against the **direction tier and the evidence**, the gaps are the four ingredients above: world-of-work information, modelling, support-building and a return loop. The recognised stance among reflective frameworks is not to withhold reality but to defer it to the person's own enquiry: Designing Your Life's prototyping "means gathering information through conversations and experiences", and Ibarra insists that "the more unfamiliar the new possibilities, the more necessary it becomes to learn about them through direct involvement rather than planning" ([HKUST syllabus](https://seng.hkust.edu.hk/sites/default/files/IMCE/UG/Course%20Syllabus/Fall_2023-2024/ENGG1400_Fall%202023-24.pdf); [Careershifters](https://www.careershifters.org/book-reviews/herminia-ibarra-working-identity)). Public tools build it in: O*NET flags Job Zones and Bright Outlook, the UK National Careers Service links every match to pay, hours and routes in ([DOL TEN 30-10](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ETA/advisories/TEN/2011/TEN30-10.pdf); [NCS explore careers](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk:443/explore-careers)). Our prompts go further than any incumbent by forbidding Sky to say whether an idea is "realistic, achievable, well paid or in demand" (`lib/newpage-tools.js:13`), which is exactly the "validates whatever I lean toward" critique levelled at general chatbots ([pigment.is](https://pigment.is/blogs/blogs/best-ai-for-career-advice)).

The **Australian context** turns these gaps into a specific opportunity. The OECD found **74% of highly educated versus 44% of low-educated mid-career Australians** had used career guidance, the widest gap in its sample, that rural adults use it less, that 20% of non-users did not know services existed, and that employed adults not at risk of job loss generally cannot access publicly subsidised guidance ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/strengthening-career-guidance-for-mid-career-adults-in-australia_e08803ce-en/full-report/)). SEEK's research finds **44% of working Australians open to a career change but only 7% acting**, with "too late" the main hesitation and 52% regretting their choice ([SEEK](https://www.seek.com.au/about/news/article/the-evolving-working-life)). A A$25 membership is roughly an eighth of one human hour and a hundredth of Bark's **~A$2,450 eight-session program**, and the plain, "a little cheeky" Australian register is built for the hesitant population the system misses. Distribution is the problem: every scaled direction tool reaches everyday people through intermediaries (CareerVillage through institutions, Anyway through schools, Getmee through providers), and the **A$312m Workforce Australia rebuild** into three streams with an Employment Goal Plan replacing Job Plans, a A$205.5m participant-led online service and early rollout from late 2026 is the channel window ([SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/jobseeker-shakeup-employment-services-changes-rishworth-2026/c3l3tfa4f); [DEWR Budget 2026-27](https://www.dewr.gov.au/download/17641/investments-reform-budget-2026-27/42874/document/pdf)). Any such channel will ask first for the privacy policy we do not have, and the OAIC's ADM transparency rules from 10 December 2026 apply with or without a channel. Mindframe's help-seeking norm (two 24/7 services with direct links) is met in-app but not on the landing page ([Mindframe](https://mindframe.org.au/mental-health/communicating-about-mental-ill-health/mindframe-guidelines)), and CICA's Guiding Principles and CDAA's GenAI guidelines are the standards provider and TAFE buyers will apply ([CICA PDF](https://cica.org.au/wp-content/uploads/CICA-Guiding-Principles-for-Career-Services-and-Informaiton-Products-3rd-ed.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The pivot from a free worksheet to a paid membership changed the question. The old Compass asked whether anyone would pay for direction-finding; the market said probably not, and venture money agreed. Sky plus the writers asks a better question: whether a coach that remembers the person, refuses to flatter them, writes nothing it cannot prove and hands them to a human when it should is worth A$25 a month against free Teal, free Indeed and a US$20 ChatGPT. The research says the coaching answer is yes, with evidence that the design choices (written, individualised, values-first, protocolised, non-sycophantic) are the ones that move outcomes, and that the hybrid model has just been vacated by BetterUp at the consumer end. The commercial answer is not yet, because the shell around the coach is what a visitor, a payments provider and a privacy regulator see first, and today it shows the wrong brand, no legal pages, an unfinished funnel and a cost curve that loses money on the members who use what they were sold.

Two moves matter more than the rest. The first is to let Sky start conversations rather than wait for them: a weekly check-in on the member's own tasks turns the memory from a privacy feature into the accountability mechanism the 2026 trials say is the active ingredient, and it is also the retention loop the lifecycle lacks. The second is to deliver reality-testing as the member's own homework rather than a verdict, so Sky can stop refusing to discuss pay and demand without becoming a worse Indeed. Do those on top of the launch blockers, and the product becomes the only Australian career coach that respects the person, remembers them, and still gets them to act; skip them, and it is an excellent coach inside a business that cannot yet safely take a card.
