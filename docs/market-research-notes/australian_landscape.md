# Australian career-guidance and career-direction landscape (as of November 2026)

Method note: research was done via web search on 2026-11-06. Direct page fetches to gov.au, seek.com.au, oecd.org, cica.org.au, twobirds.com and trade-press hosts were blocked by the session's network allowlist, so most findings rest on search-result excerpts of primary pages plus secondary coverage. Where a figure comes only from a secondary/vendor source, that is flagged. Where I could not verify a current feature set (e.g. exact Your Career quiz design in 2026), it is listed as a gap.

## Key Question 1: What free AU government tools already do values/strengths/interest exploration, how good are they, and do any use AI?

### Takeaway
Australia has two mature, free, national career-exploration tools, myfuture (ESA, 800k+ registered users, interests/skills/values profile) and Your Career (Commonwealth, Career Quiz + Skills Match), plus state equivalents (Careers NSW, Skillsroad, Jobs Tasmania Career Connector). None of the government tools is documented as using generative AI or a conversational coach; they are questionnaire-to-occupation-list matchers oriented to occupations and training, not whole-of-life direction. The first AI career coach in the AU market with real scale is the non-government Year13/"Anyway" product (14-24 year olds), and Indeed's Career Scout (job-search oriented).

### Cited Findings
**myfuture (Education Services Australia)**
- myfuture describes itself as "Australia's National Career Information Service"; it is managed by Education Services Australia, a national not-for-profit owned by state, territory and Commonwealth education ministers — [ESA project page](https://www.esa.edu.au/projects/myfuture); [Mirage News](https://www.miragenews.com/myfuture-australia-s-national-career-information-service-looks-to-future/)
- The "My career profile" tool has nine activities that help users "explore their interests, skills and values, and generate a personalised list of suggested occupations"; it can match occupations to interests, intended education level, skills and work-condition preferences — [Churchlands SHS student handout (myfuture materials)](https://churchlands.wa.edu.au/assets/documents/myfuture-information-for-students.pdf); [parent/carer handout](https://churchlands.wa.edu.au/assets/documents/myfuture-information-for-parenst-and-carers.pdf)
- Creating a profile is free — [Al Siraat College careers page](https://www.alsiraat.vic.edu.au/news/careers/myfuture-free-career-information-service)
- A 2026 trade article reports over 800,000 registered users and a "streamlined" redesign — [Technology Decisions, "Career information site streamlined"](https://www.technologydecisions.com.au/content/futureed/news/career-information-site-streamlined-634779515)
- No source found describing any AI/chat feature in myfuture; the materials describing My career profile date from roughly 2019-2022 (verification gap; myfuture.edu.au could not be fetched).

**Your Career (yourcareer.gov.au) / National Careers Institute legacy / Jobs and Skills Australia**
- Your Career was launched by the then Department of Education, Skills and Employment and delivered by the National Careers Institute; described as "Australia's authoritative source of careers information" — [PS News, 26 Sept 2023](https://psnews.com.au/?p=53126); [Ross Vasta MP](https://rossvasta.com.au/tag/career/)
- The My Skills site was folded into Your Career — [DEWR newsroom, "My Skills is now part of Your Career"](https://www.dewr.gov.au/th/newsroom/articles/my-skills-now-part-your-career)
- The site offers a short Career Quiz (interests/skills → suggested options) and a Skills Match tool that works from past jobs to show transferable skills, ideas for new careers and training gaps; a free account is required to use the tools — [myGov "Changing your career"](https://my.gov.au/en/services/education/upgrading-professional-skills-or-qualifications/changing-your-career); [JobAccess](https://jobaccess.gov.au/i-am-a-person-with-disability/looking-applying-job/getting-started-job-search/help-choose-right-job); [Storylines description of Skills Match](https://storylines.com.au/services/job-outlook/); [Melbourne Polytechnic libguide](https://libguides.melbournepolytechnic.edu.au/employmentskills/occupationssalaries)
- Jobs and Skills Australia is the statutory body providing labour-market and skills-needs advice; DEWR lists it on its "help guide your career choices" page — [DEWR](https://www.dewr.gov.au/id/skills-and-training/help-guide-your-career-choices)
- JSA's "Pathways to Success — Jobs and Skills Report 2026" (Oct 2026) highlights how better job design and career pathways can address shortages and how to make skills "easier to recognise, transfer and build upon" — [JSA report page](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/engage/about/jobs-and-skills-report-2026); [summary factsheet PDF](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-10/jobs_and_skills_report_2026_-_summary_factsheet.pdf)
- No source found describing AI features on Your Career; the Career Quiz appears to be a conventional interests quiz (gap: 2026 page could not be fetched).

**Workforce Australia (DEWR)**
- Workforce Australia Online participants and provider-serviced participants can access Career Transition Assistance (see Q3). The May 2026 reform package includes a "$205.5 million over five years for a redesigned, participant-led online service" with improved accessibility and personalisation — [DEWR Budget 2026-27 investments document](https://www.dewr.gov.au/download/17641/investments-reform-budget-2026-27/42874/document/pdf); [PS News, 28 May 2026](https://psnews.com.au/massive-overhaul-of-employment-services-system-on-its-way/179203/)

**State tools**
- Careers NSW (announced 2021, piloted via Service NSW from Oct 2021) gives free tailored guidance from a professional Career Advisor online to NSW residents 18+, plus a self-service website open to all residents and free phone advice from the NSW Skills Hub, independent advisors and industry experts (Service NSW account required) — [NSW Have Your Say media release](https://www.haveyoursay.nsw.gov.au/careers/media-release); [InnovationAus](https://innovationaus.com/?p=24973); [OECD](https://oecd.org/en/publications/strengthening-career-guidance-for-mid-career-adults-in-australia_e08803ce-en/full-report/component-6.html). Careers NSW also offers an "interactive explorer" — [careers.nsw.gov.au interactive explorer](https://pre.careers.nsw.gov.au/interactive-explorer). No 2026 funding update found.
- Skillsroad is a not-for-profit initiative of Business Australia (formerly NSW Business Chamber) offering a Career Quiz built on seven skill sets from Foundation for Young Australians research, developed with psychologists; its own disclaimer says it "does not warrant that the results of the Career Quiz are accurate" — [Skillsroad About](https://skillsroad.com.au/about-us); [Skillsroad Career Quiz](https://skillsroad.com.au/careers/career-quiz)
- Jobs Tasmania's "Career Connector" offers free advice via regional providers (Searson Buck in south/north; Youth Family and Community Connections in north-west/west) for people who lost jobs or want a next step — [Jobs Tasmania](https://www.jt.tas.gov.au/programs/career_connector)
- Victoria's Jobs Victoria Career Counsellors program (which helped disadvantaged jobseekers identify goals, pathways, skills and strengths) was defunded in the 2023-24 Victorian budget; Jobs Victoria Mentors was kept ($35.1m/2 yrs) — [VCOSS, "Victorians in work"](https://vcoss.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Victorians-in-work.pdf). The OECD had earlier called it the only publicly subsidised guidance available to employed adults not at risk of job loss — [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/strengthening-career-guidance-for-mid-career-adults-in-australia_e08803ce-en/full-report/)
- TAFE NSW offers educational and career counselling to prospective/current students — [TAFE NSW](https://tafensw.edu.au/counselling-career-development-services/educational-career-counselling); [TAFE NSW career advice](https://www.tafensw.edu.au/career-advice)

### Inferences
- The government stack is strong on information (occupation profiles, pay, training, shortage data) and on structured interests/skills/values questionnaires, but output is a list of occupations. Nothing found does what our product does: hold a conversation, let the person rank their own values, write a North Star, and commit to three small steps. That is a genuine white space, not a crowded one.
- Because every government tool requires an account (myGov/Service NSW/Your Career login), our no-account, browser-only approach is a differentiator for privacy-sensitive or low-digital-confidence users, but a weakness for return visits (see product brief gap).
- Skillsroad's own accuracy disclaimer is a useful norm: Australian quiz tools explicitly hedge. Our "never judge realism" stance should be paired with similar plain-language disclaimers.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch yourcareer.gov.au or myfuture.edu.au to confirm 2026 feature sets, number of quiz items, or whether any AI has been added in 2025-26.
- No usage statistics found for Your Career Career Quiz or Skills Match.
- No independent evaluation of the quality/validity of myfuture or Your Career matching found.

## Key Question 2: Who are the Australian private/startup players (incl. AI career coaches 2024-2026)?

### Takeaway
The only Australian-built AI career coach with scale is Year13's Career Coach, relaunched as "Anyway" in April 2026 with Microsoft backing, but it targets 14-24 year olds via schools. Getmee (Melbourne, AU$2.5m raised) is an employability/English-coaching AI being white-labelled for Y Careers. Global job boards (Indeed Career Scout, July 2026 in AU; SEEK's content and Explore Careers) dominate the adult side but are job-search-first. No Australian AI career-direction coach for mid-career "everyday" adults was found; "SEEK Pathways" and "Bloom" could not be verified as products.

## Key Question 3: Where is the unmet need for everyday/trades/care/regional workers, and what labour-market context drives demand?

### Takeaway
Demand is wide but shallow: 44% of Australians are open to a career change but only 7% are actively pursuing one, with "too late" the main hesitation, and 52% regret their career choice (SEEK). Guidance access is skewed: 74% of high-educated vs 44% of low-educated mid-career Australians have used guidance (largest gap in the OECD sample), rural adults use it less, and publicly funded adult guidance is largely restricted to the unemployed. Regional vacancy fill rates lag metro by 4.6 points and trades are the most persistent shortages, so "everyday" workers face both poor guidance access and real pathway options. The May 2026 employment-services rebuild (three streams, Employment Goal Plan, $205.5m digital service, late-2026 early rollout) creates a near-term B2B/B2G window.

### Cited Findings
**Career-change intentions**
- SEEK research (Nature, Oct 2024–Feb 2025, n=3,033 working-age Australians): 44% open to a career change, 7% actively pursuing one; main hesitation is fear it is "too late"; 52% regret their career choice and would choose differently — [SEEK, "The Evolving Working Life"](https://www.seek.com.au/about/news/article/the-evolving-working-life); [SEEK Talent report](https://talent.seek.com.au/market-insights/the-evolving-working-life-report-au)
- Robert Half (Dec 2025): 56% of Australian employees would consider changing jobs in the next year if it improved lifestyle outcomes; earlier survey found work-life balance driving over half to consider a career change — [Robert Half](https://www.roberthalf.com/au/en/about/press/1-in-2-workers-willing-to-switch-careers-in-the-next-year); [Medianet, Dec 2025](https://newshub.medianet.com.au/2025/12/half-of-australian-workers-open-to-career-change-in-2026-as-demand-for-new-skills-grows/134632)
- Indeed: 5.5 million Australians (over a third of the workforce) actively considering a job change in 2025 — [HCA Mag](https://hcamag.com/au/specialisation/financial-wellness/over-5-million-australians-eyeing-new-job-in-2025-reports/520317)

**Guidance access gaps (adults, low-educated, rural)**

- CICA (Aug 2026, National Skills Week release) says adults in regions with limited service infrastructure bear the cost of weak guidance access and calls for a National Lifelong Career Guidance Framework and a review of public funding for career guidance — [CICA release PDF](https://cica.org.au/wp-content/uploads/National-Skills-Week-highlights-the-missing-piece-in-Australias-skills-agenda-career-guidance-policy-says-peak-body.pdf)

### Inferences
- The unmet need is "direction before search": a large, hesitant population (open but not acting, fearing it is too late) that existing tools skip because they start at occupations or job ads. Our non-directive, small-steps design fits this group's hesitation profile better than a quiz-to-job-list.
- The low-educated/rural gap is where free government tools reach least; a no-account, phone-first, plain-English product is structurally suited to it, but distribution must come through trusted intermediaries (providers, TAFEs, libraries, councils) rather than organic search.
- Timing: the Employment Goal Plan is conceptually close to our Plan/First-steps/Calendar output. A provider or the DEWR digital stream could use a Compass-style map as the goal-setting front end. The late-2026 pilots and 16-month contract extension are the window.

### Gaps

## Key Question 4: Who could be B2B2C channels, and what do Australians pay human coaches / where is the value gap?

### Takeaway
Plausible channels are Workforce Australia/CTA providers (APM, MTC, ETC, AKG, MAX, atWork), TAFEs (career counselling services and Fee-Free TAFE intake), not-for-profit youth/employment orgs (Y Careers, Ladder), state services (Careers NSW, Jobs Tasmania Career Connector), unions/community orgs, local councils (which already host free career-guidance events), and outplacement firms. Human career coaching costs about A$150-300 per hour (Bark 2026 average $195/hr ex GST; Sydney ~$295), with eight-session programs around $2,450 and packages $930-$6,000, versus free but impersonal government tools; the gap is affordable, personalised reflection.

### Inferences
- A single human session costs roughly a day's pay for many award-wage workers; that is the value gap our product can fill (free or low-cost reflective coaching), while being honest that it is not a substitute for a practitioner.
- Providers are the most concrete B2B2C route: they have mandated contact with exactly the "everyday" population, are moving to Employment Goal Plans, and are already experimenting with AI coaching (MTC CoachMe, Y Careers/Getmee). A Compass map exported into a Goal Plan is a natural integration.
- Procurement reality: anything used in a mutual-obligation context attracts scrutiny over coercion and data; our non-directive, no-judgement stance is an asset, but "answers saved only in browser" will not satisfy a provider needing records. A B2B mode would need consent-based sharing.

## Key Question 5: What compliance and trust expectations would apply to an AI career-direction tool in Australia?

### Takeaway
Australia dropped mandatory AI guardrails in the National AI Plan (2 Dec 2025) in favour of technology-neutral law plus voluntary guidance and an AI Safety Institute; the binding obligations therefore come from the Privacy Act (OAIC's Oct 2024 AI guidance; statutory tort for serious privacy invasions since June 2025; automated-decision-making transparency in privacy policies from 10 Dec 2026; civil penalties up to $78,200 per unit-based tier), plus sector norms: CDAA's GenAI guidelines for practitioners, CICA's Guiding Principles for career information products, and Mindframe's help-seeking standard (list at least two 24/7 services with direct links).

### Cited Findings
**AI regulation**
- The Sept 2024 proposals paper proposed 10 mandatory guardrails for high-risk AI and an AI-specific Act — [industry.gov.au](https://industry.gov.au/news/mandatory-guardrails-safe-and-responsible-ai-have-your-say); [Proposals paper PDF](https://consultations.tga.gov.au/tga/clarifying-and-strengthening-the-regulation-of-ai/supporting_documents/proposals_paper_for_introducing_mandatory_guardrails_for_ai_in_high_risk_settings.pdf)

### Inferences
- Our product's current design (no accounts, localStorage only, chat sent to Anthropic API server-side) keeps us light on APP obligations, but once any B2B mode stores or shares a Compass map we become an APP entity handling personal (possibly sensitive, e.g. stress/health) information and must: publish a privacy policy with ADM disclosure if AI suggestions "substantially shape" anything resembling a decision (safer to disclose anyway by 10 Dec 2026), run a privacy impact assessment per OAIC's AI guide, and disclose offshore disclosure to the model provider (APP 8).
- Because the product is reflective and non-directive, it is unlikely to be "high-risk" under any revived guardrail scheme, but a mutual-obligation deployment (where outputs feed an Employment Goal Plan) would raise the stakes and attract ADM scrutiny.
- Our crisis dialog already lists more than two 24/7 services (Lifeline, Beyond Blue, 000, SANE, 13YARN) which meets the Mindframe help-seeking norm; adding direct tel:/chat links and 13YARN for First Nations users aligns with current referral sheets.
- Aligning the chat prompts with CDAA/CICA principles (client autonomy, no diagnosis, transparency that it is AI, no claims to replace a practitioner) would be a credible trust signal to provider/TAFE buyers whose staff are CDAA members.
